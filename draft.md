# What Is Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO)?

**Generative engine optimisation (GEO)** is the practice of shaping content and online presence so that AI systems — ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews — actually pull it into the answers they generate, rather than leaving it buried in a list of blue links. It's a young discipline, and it's still small enough that most of the writing about it is guesswork dressed up as certainty. This piece sticks to what's actually documented.

## What is generative engine optimisation?

Generative engine optimisation is [structuring digital content and managing your online presence to improve visibility inside AI-generated answers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_engine_optimization), rather than in a traditional list of search results. The term comes from a specific place, too: it was introduced in a [November 2023 research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) by six researchers (Aggarwal, Murahari, Rajpurohit, Kalyan, Narasimhan and Deshpande), later accepted to KDD 2024, which proposed GEO as a framework for measuring and improving how often a source gets cited inside AI-generated responses.

That origin matters, because it explains why GEO reads more like an optimisation problem than a marketing discipline in its founding form — it's about visibility *inside* a generated answer, not about earning a click.

## Is GEO replacing SEO?

No — according to Google's own guidance, the fundamentals of SEO still apply, because Google's AI features run on the same ranking systems as regular search. Google states plainly that "[the best practices for SEO continue to be relevant because our generative AI features on Google Search are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)," adding that optimising for generative AI search "is optimising for the search experience, and thus still SEO."

That's a useful corrective to a lot of GEO content that frames it as SEO's replacement. It isn't. It's closer to an added layer on top: get the SEO fundamentals right first, then structure content so it's easy for a model to lift and cite.

## What's the difference between GEO and SEO?

The two disciplines overlap more than they diverge, but the emphasis shifts. Traditional SEO optimises for ranking position and click-through; GEO optimises for whether a specific sentence gets extracted and attributed to your page inside a generated answer.

| Dimension | Traditional SEO | Generative engine optimisation |
|---|---|---|
| Primary goal | Rank high in a list of results | Get cited inside a generated answer |
| Unit that "wins" | The page (via its ranking position) | The sentence (via extraction + attribution) |
| Success signal | Click-through rate | Citation / mention rate |
| Content shape rewarded | Comprehensive pages targeting a keyword | [Structured, clearly-headed, directly-answered sections](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization) a model can lift cleanly |
| Query style it serves | Short queries (Google's average is a few words) | [Longer, conversational queries — HubSpot cites AI search queries averaging around 23 words](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization) |

## What is AEO, and how does it relate to GEO?

Answer engine optimisation (AEO) and GEO are close enough in practice that most people use them interchangeably — both describe optimising content so AI systems select it when generating an answer, and HubSpot's own definition of GEO — "[optimizing content for AI-powered search engines and answer engines that use large language models to generate conversational responses](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)" — folds "answer engines" straight into the definition of GEO.

One thing worth flagging if you're searching for this in India specifically: "AEO" on its own is a noisy search term here. It's dominated almost entirely by *Authorized Economic Operator*, a customs certification run through India's CBIC portal — not Answer Engine Optimization. If you're doing keyword research and see a huge volume number next to "AEO," check what's actually ranking before you build a page around it.

## What generative engine optimisation strategies actually work?

The tactic that comes up most consistently across the sources we checked is structure: AI systems appear to favour content that's already broken into clear, directly-answerable chunks rather than long unstructured prose. One SEO practitioner quoted by HubSpot put it plainly — "[AI-driven search tends to pull in content that's extremely structured via bulleted lists, structured headings, and general listicle-style articles](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/generative-engine-optimization)." The same piece also points to E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) signals mattering for both traditional and generative search — noting HubSpot itself has appeared as ChatGPT's top CRM recommendation while ranking third in Google's own results, which is as close to a real-world proof point as we found.

## What tools help with generative engine optimisation?

Honestly, the tooling picture here is thin, and we're not going to pretend otherwise by naming a "top 5" list we haven't tested.
